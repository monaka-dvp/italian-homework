The common thread. sar- is one stem with two sets of endings — futuro and condizionale. si- runs the whole congiuntivo presente, and its -iamo / -iate endings are the same for every verb in the language, so they're worth learning once.

One m separates the two tenses. saremo = we will be · saremmo = we would be. Same stakes for voi, but a different letter: sarete (will) vs sareste (would) — an added s, not a doubled m.
siete or siate? Sei libero domani? — a plain question. Spero che tu sia libero domani. — after a trigger word (spero / penso / credo che…). What decides it is the trigger, not the meaning.
The top three congiuntivo rows are identical. sia / sia / sia — so Penso che sia covers io, tu and lui. It feels underspecified; it isn't.
Imperfetto takes none of the regular endings. Other verbs go -vo -vi -va (facevo, bevevo). essere has its own set — ero, eri, era — and only noi and voi show a v at all (eravamo, eravate).
siamo does triple duty — presente, congiuntivo and imperativo, all noi. So do siate (congiuntivo + imperativo) and sia (congiuntivo + the polite Lei command).
