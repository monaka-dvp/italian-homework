Partitivo — how to say “some”

Written 2026-08-17, on your own request, after G-024 came back 1/4 cold. The good news is in that result: you already reach for di + article without being told. Only the choosing is missing, and it is one move.

The whole thing in one move

Ask what article the noun already takes. Then glue di onto it.
il pane → del pane · le patate → delle patate · gli amici → degli amici.
That is the entire rule. There is nothing to memorise separately — if you know the article, you know the partitive.

Your actual error, named

On 2026-08-17 you wrote dei bambini ✓, then degli for bambine, fiori and macchine.

degli is the narrowest of the three plurals, not the general one. It exists only where the noun would take gli — i.e. a masculine plural starting with a vowel or an awkward cluster. Everything else splits simply:

“But how do I know the noun’s gender?” — the missing step

Added 2026-08-17, straight after he asked “why dei for fiori? is this masculine?” — a fair question, and the page above assumed an answer it never gave. Yes: il fiore is masculine.
singular ends ingenderexample
-omasculineil libro → i libri → dei libri
-afemininela casa → le case → delle case
-eeither — you cannot tell; it is learned with the wordil fiore (m) · la chiave (f)
The trap, and he produced the proof himself the same morning. Both of these make their plural in -i:
il fiore → i fiori (masculine) · la chiave → le chiavi (feminine).
He wrote «Le mie chiavi sono sparite» correctly — so a plural in -i is not a sign of masculine.

The heuristic that does hold: a plural in -e is nearly always feminine (it came from -a). A plural in -i is usually masculine — but if the singular ended in -e, check.

Two families that are always feminine: -zione / -sione (la stazione · l’informazione) and -tà (la città · la libertà).

Two different “some”s

meaningexample
pluralsome / a few — things you can countHo comprato delle mele. — I bought some apples.
singularsome / a bit of — things you can’t countVuoi del vino? — Do you want some wine?
English uses one word for both. Italian uses the same construction for both — so this is easier than it looks, not harder.

The alternatives, and the one trap in them

formuseexample
alcuni / alcune+ plural noun. Interchangeable with dei/delle most of the time; slightly more “a few”.alcuni amici · alcune amiche
qualche⚠ always followed by a SINGULAR noun, even though it means “some/a few”. This is the trap.qualche amico = some friends (not qualche amici)
un po’ diuncountable only — “a bit of”. No article after di.un po’ di pane

When to leave it out

In negatives, drop it. Non ho pane. — not non ho del pane.
Bare plural is also fine and very common: Ho comprato patate is natural Italian. The partitive adds “some”; leaving it out is not an error.

So the risk here is small: using it correctly sounds good, and leaving it out sounds fine. The only thing that sounds wrong is choosing the wrong one — which is the single step this page exists to fix.
